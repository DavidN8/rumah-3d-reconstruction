### Q2. How should overlapping temporal zones be fused?
**Decision:** Look at why `F20` (Corridor/bed, p. 10) and `F22` (Bedroom entrance, p. 11) have torn ghost sheets and double walls:
1. `zone-1` (`frames 1389–4090`) and `zone-2` (`frames >= 4091`) come from the **exact same continuous COLMAP component (the 325-frame living+corridor+bedroom model)**!
2. Splitting a single 325-frame component temporally at `frame 4091` caused `zone-1` cameras (e.g., `frame 3606` looking through the bedroom door) to reconstruct a low-density outer shell of the bedroom, while `zone-2` cameras reconstructed the interior of the bedroom—resulting in **two overlapping meshes fighting over the same doorway and bedroom walls**!
**Action:**
- **Option A (Preferred for Living + Bedroom):** Since `zone-1` + `zone-2` are only **325 registered frames total** in a single rigid COLMAP component (fewer frames than Samsung's 410-frame bedroom!), **do NOT split `zone-1` and `zone-2` into separate temporal zones!** Train the **Living + Corridor + Bedroom (`325` frames, minus transient swinging-door frames)** as **ONE unified zone (`zone-living-bedroom`, `cap-max 3,000,000`, `quality high`, `50,000` steps)**! That completely eliminates the `zone-1 ↔ zone-2` seam, eliminates duplicate overlapping bedroom/corridor walls, and eliminates the `zone-2` floor-tilt discrepancy!
- **For the remaining `Kitchen (zone-0) ↔ Living-Bedroom` doorway seam:** Use **3D bounding-box / doorway-plane clipping with occlusion-aware depth-buffer support** (a triangle in `zone-0` that lies on the Living Room side of the doorway plane and is behind `zone-1`'s rendered depth buffer from `zone-1` cameras is clipped cleanly along the doorway plane, exactly like Samsung's roofless plane clipper on p. 16!).

- When `zone-1` and `zone-2` are unified into a single `zone-living-bedroom` training run (per Q2), this artificial split disappears completely.

### Q6. Can geometry be preserved within the delivery budget?
**Decision:** Look at p. 7 (Panel A vs Panel B) and p. 16 (Samsung):
- Re-extracting with `--max-grid-res 384 --merge-factor 1.2` damaged the geometry *during occupancy/Delaunay extraction* before textures were even baked.
- Instead:
  1. Extract with `--merge-factor 0.6` (or `0.5`) and default/high grid resolution so thin walls, floors, and appliances are extracted faithfully without grid-starvation holes.
  2. With **2 zones instead of 3** (`Zone 0: Kitchen` + `Zone 1: Unified Living/Corridor/Bedroom`), you only have **two `8192×8192` textures (`glb+jpeg90`)** instead of three (immediately saving 33% texture payload!).
  3. If the raw triangle count across the 2 zones is ~3M–5M triangles and you want ~1.5M–2.0M triangles for `< 100 MB` fast delivery, apply **UV-seam-aware, error-bounded quadric mesh simplification (`quadric_decimation` preserving UV boundaries and planar walls with max geometric error $\le 2\text{ mm}$)** OR `--merge-factor 0.65` with `--max-grid-res 640` (verify on the controlled render that **zero** new floor/wall holes appear compared to the un-simplified extraction!).
